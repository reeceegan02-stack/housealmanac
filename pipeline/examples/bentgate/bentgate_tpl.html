The story of your street

Bentgate Street is a mill street. In 1882 the Garfield Spinning Company built a cotton mill here, beside the River Beal, with 61,364 spindles spinning medium to super-fine yarn. Ten years later Ellenroad Mill went up just to the north. Both are on the map above.

Newhey grew up around its mills. In 1828 it was described as “several ranges of cottages and two public houses”. The railway arrived in 1863, St Thomas’ church followed in 1876, and by 1911 Newhey’s ten cotton mills employed more than 2,000 people.

Garfield Mill was demolished in 1969, and Ellenroad in 1985, all but its engine house, now Ellenroad Steam Museum. The fields south of the street on the old map are now houses, and number 78, first sold new in 2003, is one of the newest homes on a street the Ordnance Survey was already naming in 1907.

The same streets today. Ellenroad’s engine house still stands as a steam museum, and the M62 now runs past to the west.
