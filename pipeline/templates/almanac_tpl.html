The story of your street

Before it was Anita Street, it was Sanitary Street. Model homes were built here shortly after Victoria Square went up across the road in 1889–94 — the first council housing in Manchester.

This was mill country: Paragon and Sedgewick cotton mills stood a few streets away. By 1815 Ancoats was already Manchester’s most populous district; in 1851 it was home to 53,737 people.

A century on, residents had had enough of the name. In late 1951 they asked the council to change it, and by the spring of 1952 the street was Anita Street. Look closely: the new name was hiding in the old one — drop the S and the “-ry”.

The same streets today. Victoria Square still stands, and the mills are homes and offices.
